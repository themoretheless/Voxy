# Candidate experimental calibration source

Moura et al., *Experimental characterization of the non-linear viscoelastic properties of human female perineal tissue*, Scientific Reports (2025), [article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26576-y).

The article reports stress-relaxation testing of post-mortem perineal-body specimens, with a 900-second observation window after cyclic conditioning. This is a different tissue and protocol from the synthetic wall/support benchmark. Its coefficients cannot be assigned to skin or all genital tissues merely by association.

The article links [dataset DOI 10.5281/zenodo.15407814](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15407814). On 2026-10-02 the public record API reports `access_right=restricted` and `files=[]`. `zenodo-record.json` preserves the retrieved metadata; `provenance.json` records its checksum and access status. No experimental raw curve has been downloaded, no fit performed, and no coefficients installed. License metadata alone does not grant access to unavailable files.

Next validation requires accessible numerical observations, exact experimental strain history and specimen geometry, plus compatibility between the published constitutive convention and the implementation. A normalized relaxation trend alone cannot identify bulk modulus, anisotropy, spatial layer properties or all Maxwell branches.

## Published-model compatibility check

[Full article in PMC, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12660666/#Tab1) supplies three fitted cases and their paired relaxation times: minimum 49.73/1189.62 s, mean 31.75/334.03 s, maximum 130.76/1333.56 s. These case names are the publication's labels, not confidence bounds. `published-timescale-diagnostic.json` preserves the times and computes ideal exponential branch retention at 2.3 and 900 s. The calculated retention is not an experimental total-stress curve.

| Requirement | Published formulation | Current viscous implementation | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elastic response | Anisotropic HGO | Isotropic isochoric Ogden | Elastic coefficients cannot be copied directly |
| Viscous response | Generalized Maxwell coupled to HGO | Reference-strain Maxwell branches with backward Euler retention | Finite-strain history/stress conventions need separate verification |
| Volume response | Incompressible experimental stress conversion | Finite bulk penalty | No bulk modulus identified by this source |
| Conditioning | Experimental cycles before hold; published viscous fit excludes those cycles | Fresh reference in synthetic benchmark | Distinguish full experiment replay from the authors' relaxation-only fit |

Implementation evidence: `crates/physics/src/biomechanics/viscoelastic.rs`, `strain`, `retention`, `response`, and `advance`. The physiological source is relevant to a future perineal material profile; it does not establish equivalence of the existing law. Published relaxation times are archived for comparison only. No production material defaults changed.

## Equation transcription check

Visually inspected the publisher-hosted equation images linked by PMC. Equation 5 displays `k1/(2*k2) * (exp((I4bar-1)^2)-1)` with no visible `k2` in the exponent. A conventional HGO expression instead has `exp(k2*(I4bar-1)^2)`. These are different laws; neither substitution nor an assertion that this is an author error is justified without further evidence. Parameter headers and the implemented UMAT convention still need verification.

Equation 9 displays an exponential stress-memory convolution driven by an elastic stress-rate contribution. That differs from assuming direct equivalence to our reference-strain Maxwell history. Exact stress contributions and internal-variable definitions must be resolved before implementation. Section “Calibration” explicitly states that cyclic preloading was excluded from the viscous fit; the earlier conditioning requirement is therefore qualified above. No coefficients were installed and no claim of reproducing the authors' model is made.
